A citation-integrity layer for legal documents

Does this case actually say that?

A pipeline that checks every citation in a legal brief against the real case text — catching fabricated citations and misquoted holdings before a filing goes out.

This is the actual, validated output of the system running against a real test brief — twelve citations, checked one by one against a 58-case corpus of real UK judgments, with the exact verdict each one received.

How a citation gets checked

  1. ExtractEvery citation in the document is pulled out and paired with the case name mentioned beside it.
  2. Check the local libraryFuzzy-matched against a corpus of real case law. Found locally, or not — this alone is never enough to call something fake.
  3. Retrieve the real passageFor a matched case, hybrid search (semantic + keyword) finds the specific passage the brief is relying on.
  4. Judge the claimAn LLM checks whether that passage actually supports what the brief says — grounded strictly in the retrieved text, not its own memory of the case.
  5. Check the wider worldAnything not found locally gets a live web-search check before it's ever called fabricated — the corpus is known to be incomplete, and two real cases prove it.

Built on real, messy data

The underlying corpus is 58 real UK case law files — about a quarter of them scanned images requiring OCR, two of them accidentally duplicated, two of them .docx hiding inside .zip files. The pipeline is built to handle that mess, not a clean synthetic dataset.
